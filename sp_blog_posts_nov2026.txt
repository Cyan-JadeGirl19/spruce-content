TITLE: What a Clogged Gutter Actually Does to Your House
SLUG: what-clogged-gutters-do-to-your-house
META: Clogged gutters dump rain beside your foundation. Here is what that costs, the signs to look for, and when to call a crew in Greenville, SC.

BODY:
A typical roof sheds about 500 gallons of water in a one inch rain. That is not a typo. Now picture all of it pouring over the front edge of a gutter that is packed with wet leaves, instead of running out the downspout.

That water has to go somewhere. It usually goes exactly where you do not want it.

The water hits the ground right beside your foundation. Soil gets soft, then it gets muddy, then it moves. Around Greenville that red clay holds water like a bowl. Over a season or two you get settling, cracks in a slab, or a crawl space that stays damp and smells like it.

Before that happens, the gutter itself starts failing. Water sits in the trough and the seams start to leak. The fascia board behind the gutter stays wet and starts to rot. Paint below the gutter peels in long strips. In summer, the standing water becomes a mosquito nursery.

You can spot most of this from the yard. Look for these things after the next good rain:

Water spilling over the front edge, especially at the corners.
Dark streaks running down the siding below the trough.
Plants or little green sprouts growing out of the gutter.
A gutter front that looks bent or sagging in spots.

Any one of those means the gutter is not moving water, it is holding it.

Cleaning gutters is simple work but it is not easy work. Everything has to be scooped out by hand. The downspouts get flushed with a hose until water runs clear at the bottom. If a section holds water after the rain stops, the slope is wrong and needs a small adjustment. Then the mess gets bagged and hauled off, not left in your flower beds.

Can you do it yourself? On a one story house, on a dry day, with someone holding the ladder steady, honestly, yes. We would rather you be safe than sorry. Two stories changes the math. That is ladder work on slick, wet debris with your weight reaching past the rails, and it is where most of the ladder injuries we hear about happen.

It is November and the trees are just about done dropping. If your gutters are doing the waterfall thing, or you cannot remember the last time anyone looked at them, that is the sign. TITLE: How Gutter Guards Work (and When They Don't)
SLUG: how-gutter-guards-work-greenville-sc
META: Gutter guards use water surface tension, not a filter. Here is how they work, what still gets through, and whether they make sense for your house.

BODY:
Most people think a gutter guard is a strainer, like the one in your kitchen sink. It is not. The good ones work because of something you can test at the kitchen table right now.

Fill a glass with water. Take a butter knife and hold it at a slight angle, then touch the curve of it to the water's surface and pull. The water clings to the steel and follows it around the bend instead of dripping straight down. That is surface tension.

A curved gutter guard uses the same trick. Rain runs across the guard and clings to its rounded nose, wraps around, and drops into the gutter through a slot on the underside. Leaves and pine needles cannot turn that corner. They ride across the top, dry off, and blow away. The debris never touches the water.

It is simple physics, and it works. Here is the honest part, though.

Nothing is zero maintenance. Small stuff still gets through the opening over time: shingle grit, seed pods, the fine stuff. Pines around Greenville are relentless, and needles can catch in the corner where the guard meets the roof. So the system still wants a look every year or two. The difference is what the look involves. Instead of scooping handfuls of rot, we rinse and check. Ten minutes, not an afternoon.

Guards also will not rescue a bad gutter. If the run is out of slope, or the downspouts are too small for the roof, a guard just makes clean water fail in a new way. That is why we check the whole system before we quote guards. Sometimes the gutter needs a small fix first. Anyone who sells you guards without looking at your gutters is selling you a box, not a solution.

So who are guards actually for? If you are paying for cleaning more than once a season, or you have gutters over a bed of shrubs you are tired of us stomping through, they pay for themselves fast. If you are comfortable on a ladder and your trees are maples that drop once in the fall, maybe you clean them yourself and skip it.

TITLE: Those Cloudy Spots on Your Windows Are Not Dirt
SLUG: hard-water-spots-on-windows-greenville-sc
META: Sprinkler water leaves minerals that etch glass. Why Windex fails, when polishing still works, and the ten minute fix that stops it.

BODY:
Every summer we get the same call. The homeowner just washed their own windows, the whole family helped, and the spots are still there. You can feel them with a fingernail. A rag does nothing. Windex does nothing.

Here is what is going on. Those spots came from your sprinklers, or from irrigation next door. When a spritz of water dries on glass, the water leaves but the minerals in it do not. Our water around Greenville runs mineral heavy, which is why this is such a common window complaint here. Calcium and lime stay behind as a tiny ring. After enough sprays you get the cloudy crust you are looking at now.

Here is the part that matters. That crust is not sitting on the glass. It is slowly eating into it. Glass is softer than people think, and mineral deposits etch the surface a little more every month they sit. Fresh spots polish out easily. Spots that have baked through a few summers may never come all the way off, no matter what we do.

This is why scrubbing harder is the worst plan. Household cleaners are made for grease and dust, not minerals. You will smear the etching around, put fine scratches in the glass, and the spots will still be there when the sun comes out and lights them all up again.

The real fix is polishing. We use a compound and a machine, and it works like polishing a scratch out of a car. The compound knocks the mineral deposit down level with the glass. Done right, the window goes back to clear, and it is genuinely satisfying to watch. Sills and tracks get cleaned while we are there, because spotted glass with filthy sills is half a job.

The cheaper fix is prevention, and you can do it this weekend. Find the sprinkler heads that hit the house and turn them a few degrees toward the yard. Ten minutes with a screwdriver and the problem stops growing. Do it before the glass gets bad and you may never need us at all.

If the spots are already white and crusty, do not wait another season. Every month they sit, they dig in a little deeper. TITLE: The Black Streaks on Your Roof Are Alive
SLUG: black-roof-streaks-algae-greenville-sc
META: Dark roof streaks are a living algae feeding on your shingles. Why rain will not wash it off, why pressure makes it worse, and what actually works.

BODY:
Drive through any neighborhood around Greenville and you will see the same thing on house after house. Dark streaks running down the roof, like soot someone smeared below the ridge line. Most owners assume it is dirt, or mold from a leak, or shade grime.

It is none of those. It is alive.

The streaks are a bacteria called Gloeocapsa magma. It rides in on the wind, lands on your roof, and settles in to eat. Its favorite food is the limestone filler inside your shingles, which is a real ingredient in modern roofing. The dark color is the bacteria's own sun shield. The streaks grow downhill because that is how it spreads, feeding as it goes, one rain at a time.

Rain does not wash it off. If rain cleaned roofs, the streaks would not exist in one of the rainiest states in the country. The bacteria holds on tight, and every rain gives it a little more moisture to work with.

So people reach for the pressure washer. Please do not, and do not hire anyone who does. Those streaks are not a stain sitting on top of your shingles. The colony is embedded in the surface. Blast it with pressure and you will strip the granules, the sandy armor that keeps your shingles waterproof and reflects the sun. You get a roof that looks better for a few weeks and ages years in one afternoon. Some manufacturers will void a warranty over it.

The right way is slower and smarter. Soft washing uses plain water pressure, less than a garden hose, and does the work with a cleaning mix instead. The mix kills the bacteria at the root, down in the shingle, instead of just blowing the top layer off. The streaks fade over the following weeks as the dead algae washes out. Done right, the roof stays clean for years, because the food source is gone, not shoveled to the next yard.

It also matters more than looks. An appraiser, a buyer, and an insurance adjuster all read a streaked roof the same way, as a roof on its way out. A clean roof reads as a maintained house.
